Fifteens edition of Sodi World Series Finals starts on Wednesday in LeMans

19.05.2026, Uldis Galdiņš


On Wednesday, May 20, the 2026 Sodi World Series World Finals in rental karting will begin with official practice sessions, followed later in the afternoon by qualifying heats, at one of the most legendary venues in the world of motorsport — Le Mans. Latvia will have a record-breaking representation this year, with 13 drivers competing across all SWS categories — Sprint Cup, Junior Cup (both Junior and Kids subclasses), Masters Cup, Women Cup, and the crown jewel of rental karting, the 24-hour Endurance final. Our Lithuanian and Estonian Openkart communities are also represented more strongly than in previous years.

More about the drivers and the history a little later, but first, the schedule for this week. Everything starts on Wednesday with official free practice sessions for the non-junior categories — Sprint, Masters, and Women. Later in the afternoon, each participant will complete three qualifying races that will determine their starting positions for the following day's three heats. In the sprint categories, super heats and finals will take place on Saturday. At the same time, unofficial practice sessions for the junior categories will be held at the neighboring and slightly smaller Alain Prost karting circuit.

Friday will be a rest day for Sprint Cup drivers, while the main track will host the first-ever 24-hour Endurance race in SWS Finals history (traditionally, the World Finals featured a 12-hour race). Meanwhile, the junior categories will decide their battles at the Prost circuit, with everything being settled in a single day. On the final day, the 24-hour race will finish in the morning, followed by the decisive races in all Sprint categories.

Number of participants:

  • Sprint Cup — 105
  • Masters Cup — 35
  • Women Cup — 35
  • Junior Cup — 30
  • Kid Cup — 30
  • Endurance Cup — 48 teams (4 drivers per team)

In total, 427 drivers from all over the world are expected. Of course, as every year, some will not arrive, but this is still the largest field in SWS history — a record number of competitors battling over four days to determine the best rental karting drivers in the world.

The Sodi World Series global ranking system was created in 2009 as a karting development program, while the first World Finals took place in Paris in 2011. Latvia has been represented every year since 2016.

Latvia's greatest success has come through Andrejs Laipnieks, who finished in the top six of the Sprint Cup four consecutive times and achieved a podium finish on July 17, 2021, in Lonato, Italy, crossing the line third in the Super Final. Two years later in Slovakia, Andrejs came close to becoming world champion after starting the Super Final from pole position, but a massive crash caused by impatient drivers in the middle of the pack forced him to retire in the second corner. Many still remember that incident today.

Representing a Polish endurance team in 2024, Andrejs returned to Sprint competition last year in Campillos, Spain, finishing 10th. This will be his tenth participation in the SWS World Finals, making him one of the most experienced rental karting drivers in the world.

Latvia will also be represented in Sprint Cup by Dāvis Zalmans — a five-time Openkart vice-champion who has competed in the SWS Finals five times already and achieved remarkably consistent results, finishing in the Top 30 four consecutive years. Considering the final features around 100 of the best drivers in the world — while tens of thousands attempt to qualify — this is an impressively strong achievement.

Latvia will also have two Sprint Cup debutants: experienced Openkart drivers Ričards Alekss Švaglis and Sergejs Ļesiks. Both have come close to Openkart victories, though they are still chasing their first win. The same applies to Ruslans Dzenis, who is representing Latvia in the Masters Cup for the second consecutive year. Despite many successes in rental karting, he is still searching for that elusive first Openkart victory.

For the fourth consecutive year, Latvia will also be represented in the Women Cup. Alise Štolcermane needs little introduction — not only because of her surname and her motorsport family background, but mainly because of what she is capable of achieving in rental karting despite her roots in professional racing karting.

Among the juniors, this will be the second World Finals appearance for Otīlija Grandberga, who finished in the overall Top 5 of the annual rankings last year. It will also be her final season competing as a junior. Last year in Campillos she delivered several brilliant races, though a mix of good and bad fortune prevented an even stronger result. This year is expected to be not only a fitting conclusion to her junior SWS career, but also preparation for the Women Sprint Cup, where she is currently the leading contender for Latvia's qualification spot.

Latvia also has two more junior representatives — Ādams Šics from Sigulda and Gustavs Narvaišs from Cēsis — both of whom first discovered karting at the Klikk Center in Sigulda. Both are young, both are making their debut, and both have already moved into competitive racing karting. Most likely, both will compete in the Junior Kids category.

A brief explanation of how the 60 finalists are divided between Junior and Junior Kids:
First, all participants must be at least 11 years old at the time of the event. The older juniors are classified as Juniors if they are still 14 years old when the qualification year begins. Sprint and Endurance categories are open to drivers who turned 15 or older during the previous year. It may sound complicated at first, but becomes clear once you look into it. Ultimately, placement into Junior or Junior Kids is determined by only one factor — driver weight. The 30 lightest drivers compete in Junior Kids, while the 30 heavier drivers race in Junior. We will know where Ādams and Gustavs will compete on Wednesday morning.

And finally — the crown discipline of rental karting: the Endurance Cup. Endurance racing is hugely popular worldwide, especially in Europe, and is also rapidly growing in Latvia and the Baltics. For the first time in SWS history, the Finals will feature what endurance racing truly should be — a full 24-hour race instead of only 12 hours. There is simply no comparison between the two; nothing comes close, and rental karting is the perfect platform to experience it.

Latvia will be represented by the leading Baltic endurance team, Movers.lv. Latvia still remains the only Baltic country to have representation in this category at the SWS Finals, and this will already be the fourth time.

Latvia's story in 24-hour endurance karting — where not only drivers but also strategists and managers play a crucial role — began in 2015 at the prestigious Dubai 24-hour race. Since then, a core group of drivers, now led by the Movers.lv team, has continued competing in endurance races across Europe while also developing and promoting this discipline in Latvia and the Baltics.

The Movers.lv team consists of four drivers:

  • Kaspars Ritums — the ideological engine and driving force behind endurance racing in Latvia, one of the most experienced Openkart drivers ever, with a special passion for endurance racing;
  • Krišs Jaunzemis — a two-time Openkart champion and experienced 24-hour racer;
  • Henrijs Grūbe — once one of Latvia's brightest karting talents, closely associated with the Kartings.lv Indoor Karting Hall, who has successfully transitioned into endurance rental karting after his professional racing career;
  • Kristofers Caune — a young driver who already has experience in both the SWS Sprint Cup World Finals and various endurance races.

Lithuania will also have three drivers at the SWS Finals:

  • Orestas Burgaila — Sprint Cup
  • Meda Mickeliunaite — Women Cup
  • Jokubas Kaminskas — Junior Cup

Estonia, whose karting scene has become very active over the past two years both at home and in Latvia, will have five representatives:

  • Ilya Udre — Sprint Cup
  • Kristian Salvet — Sprint Cup
  • Aleksandr Svjatnoi — Sprint Cup
  • Hanna Grete Suvemaa — Women Cup
  • Cameron Korkka — Junior Cup

That means a total of 21 Baltic drivers can in one way or another be considered part of the Openkart community, having raced not only at home but also on Latvian circuits.

e-Sprint Cup Finals

In June, Sicily will also host the World Finals for electric Sodi karts. This will be the fourth such global event, reflecting the growth of electric rental karting, especially in indoor venues. Thanks to the activity of the Sigulda Klikk Center and enthusiasts from Sigulda and surrounding regions, Latvia has been represented at every final so far.

This year Latvia will once again have record representation:

  • Edijs Maļinovskis
  • Edvards Keziks
  • Sofija Anna Gisiča
  • Māris Liepiņš

have qualified for the e-Sprint Cup, while Ādams Šics and Gustavs Narvaišs qualified in the junior category.

Until now, Andrejs Laipnieks had been the only Latvian to compete in both the combustion-engine and electric karting world finals. Movers.lv junior team drivers Ādams and Gustavs now have every opportunity to repeat that achievement.
